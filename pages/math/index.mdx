## Overview

This page tries to explain to a layman why math is interesting and
important. First it describes what the math is that we learn through
high school and college, then what "real" math actually is. Then we try
to explain why that "real math" is very interesting, as opposed to the
"school math".

## School Math is not "Real" Math

School math is mainly involving numbers and
adding/subtracting/multiplying/dividing the numbers. At some point you
learn to deal with variables, but still revolving around numbers. Then
you might get into calculus, where you deal with super small numbers. If
you go into engineering or just beyond calculus, you might get into
matrices and stuff like that, but it all involves numbers, too.

This makes us think that **math is numbers**. But this is not true. Math
is not just numbers.

## "Real" Math

"Real" math is what you learn in graduate school or as a math major with
a few years of weeder courses under your belt. Real math involves
"objects", "collections" of objects, and "relations" among those objects
and collections. One important class of these objects is "functions",
which take input objects and transform them to output objects.

Real math is interesting when you study
[Group Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_theory), which
teaches you one modern and mainstream foundation to math. It teaches you
about [symmetries](/math/symmetries) within collections. Basically it
teaches you about patterns, not necessarily using numbers (though
numbers can be an easy/familiar example to work with).
